# Story Synopsis (draft for review)

## Premise

Hercules is the strongest man alive, and he is trying to outwork something he won't talk about. He serves a king who is terrified of him, and the king keeps moving the goalposts. The film is a dark comedy about a man trapped in a system that cannot be satisfied, told in chapters named for the people the work touches. The family catastrophe that put him in service is withheld, then shown in pieces that change each time they return, and presented in full late.

## Chapters (presentation order)

1. **Eurystheus.** Hercules is already in service. He carries the Nemean Lion into a throne room where the king hides in a storage jar and rules from inside it. The king announces ten tasks, then home. A thumbprint in a hide no weapon could scratch shows the king exactly how afraid to be, and he decides the next task will be harder.
2. **Iolaus.** Hercules's nephew burns the stumps as the Hydra's heads regrow, and they win. The king rules it void: Hercules had help. The first goalpost moves, and Iolaus carries the cost with sarcasm.
3. **Hera.** At an inn Hercules says Hera has been at him since the cradle. She is at the table, careless and entertained, and she and Athena tell it, bickering over the details. She thinks of all of it as a small matter.
4. **The Farm Wife.** The Hind takes a year of chasing. A woman who has fed hunter and hind asks where his wife is, and he leaves without answering. The first fragment of what he won't discuss: a house he can't enter, and a smell.
5. **The Herdsman.** Hercules diverts two rivers through a machine of gates and channels to clean the Augean stables. The king rules it void again, because the owner promised payment. Ten becomes twelve, and it starts to look like the number will never stop.
6. **Athena.** Birds and Bull go quickly. Athena hands over bronze clappers. At the Bull, Hercules refuses Minos's help, having taken the wrong lesson from the Hydra: help invalidates the work.
7. **Abderus.** At the Mares, Hercules leaves a young man who trusts him in charge, and the mares eat him. The fragment returns and now includes this failure, so it accuses.
8. **Hippolyta.** She agrees to give her belt. Hera spreads a lie, Hercules believes it, and Hippolyta dies in the fight. Force has made things worse.
9. **Megara.** The family event arrives in full, around 70 percent in: the life they built, and the day Hera walked in, saw what her scheme became, and walked out aghast. The camera stays with Megara. After this chapter the jokes stop around her and return everywhere else.
10. **Geryon.** Hercules herds living cattle across the world while Hera disrupts the road. He doesn't win by force.
11. **Prometheus.** At the rock Hercules frees him. Prometheus has greeted the eagle each morning with "Good morning, old friend," until the morning it doesn't come.
12. **Atlas.** Hercules outthinks the titan, who takes the sky back.
13. **Theseus.** Hercules goes unarmed into the Underworld, frees Theseus, cannot free Pirithous, and for the first time accepts help. Cerberus goes back up.
14. **Hercules.** Service is over, but the king tries to add a thirteenth task [open: a task past the oracle's mandate, the restored house as the system's last impossible clause, or Hercules's own act afterward]. Hercules sees that no count will ever be enough, because the debt was never the king's to collect. He walks out and chooses to keep living. He is not wiser or absolved.

## Running threads

- **The gods are careless, not cruel.** They treat his troubles as gossip, and their consequence for what they did is [open: an act such as Hercules wounding Hera, an enforced witness, an ensemble job on Olympus, or fallout that arrives unforced].
- **The legend inflates** in every town that tells it, long after the real man has stopped recognizing it. [open: placements and payoff]
- **Prometheus and his eagle** keep time for the film: the eagle lands each dawn until it doesn't.
- **Humor** comes from the powerful and the procedural. Grief and the dead are never the butt of a joke.
- **Hercules is messy**, not moralized. Earlier harms are not cancelled by later restraint.
