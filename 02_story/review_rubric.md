# Story Review Rubric

Status: Draft v2. Calibrated on one scene pair and one synopsis run (`reviews/`). It has not shown it can tell good writing from great. The panel procedure lives in `skills/review-hercules-script/SKILL.md`.

Reviews inform the user's decisions and never approve anything. "Approved" still requires the user's explicit record (`PROJECT.md`). The user is the taste authority.

## What this process can and cannot show

- **Can show:** where a cold reader is confused or bored, whether a scene turns, whether voices are distinct, whether a boundary is crossed, whether a beat can be staged in clay, whether the causal chain holds, and where a pitch loses a listener.
- **Cannot show:** that the writing is good or that the film is enjoyable. Model readers are weak at real laughter and real emotion. They flatter, converge, pad findings to fill a template, and sometimes assert wrong facts about the myth. Treat output as instrument readings, require quotes, verify source claims, and expect noise.
- **Real tests:** table reads with real people, and the G010 animatic. Collect human reactions on a few scenes early and compare them with the panel's. If they disagree often, discount the panel.

## Three levels, three different review sets

The same lenses do not fit every level. A synopsis cannot show whether dialogue is good, and a scene cannot show whether the story is.

| Level | Input | Questions it can answer | Questions it cannot |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Story** (synopsis, beat sheet, treatment) | Chapter-by-chapter summary | Does it pull? Is the chain causal? Does the premise pay off? Are setups paid? Is there variety? Does it read as a thesis? | Are the jokes funny? Do voices differ? Is the scene alive? |
| **Sequence** (a chapter or run of scenes) | The scenes in order | Pacing, variety of shape and tone, chapter turn, setup and payoff inside the chapter | Whether the whole film works |
| **Scene** | Pages | Turn, want, voice, subtext, physical event, comic engine, stageability, boundary check | Whether it earns its place in the whole |

Passing at one level does not predict passing at another. A strong synopsis can hold weak scenes, and strong scenes can sit in a shapeless story.

## Boundaries and intentions (guarding against heavy-handed writing)

Declarations in the decision log are of two kinds. Mixing them is how a story ends up demonstrating its own rules.

- **Boundaries** are things that must not happen (for example, no humor at the expense of victims; the family catastrophe is not stated in the opening movements). They are checked by an auditor as "violated or not". Boundaries are never a reason to add anything to a scene.
- **Intentions** are things we hope the work achieves (for example, that comedy comes from character, that the gods are careless). They live in the writer's brief in plain language. They are never checked by quoting rule numbers and are never required to appear in every scene.

Rules that follow:

1. **Write from the scene card and the intent, not from the decision log.** Decision numbers never appear in screenplay pages.
2. **Auditors see boundaries only.** Auditors check for violations. They do not score how well a scene "expresses" a decision.
3. **A scene is not required to serve a motif.** At most one beat in a scene may carry theme. A scene that exists mainly to demonstrate an idea fails the heavy-hand test.
4. **Tests are a floor, not a target.** A scene that passes every test and has no life still fails. Reviewers always answer two open questions: the most alive moment, and the deadest moment.
5. **Heavy-hand test (every level).** Does anything announce its own meaning? Does a motif get explained instead of felt? Is the same mechanism (a ruling moves the goalposts, a character misreads) repeated until it is formula? Does the text tell the audience how to feel? A surprising, specific human detail should outweigh a stated theme.
6. **Keep some reviewer questions unpublished to the writer** (the open questions in rule 4, and the pitch-reader questions) so writing is not tuned to the checklist.
7. **Comedy is not a quota.** No jokes-per-page measure. Humor is judged only for whether it comes from character and situation and whether it lands.

## Functional and enjoyable

Both must hold. They are measured differently.

### Functional (does it work?)

| Instrument | How | Reads |
|---|---|---|
| Retell | A cold reader retells the scene or story in three sentences | Clarity: a wrong retelling means unclear writing |
| Scene card inference | Reviewer states purpose, entering condition, turn, exit | Compared with the writer's card |
| Turn / want / delete tests | Rubric scene tests | Whether the scene earns its place |
| Chronology reconstruction | Cold reader rebuilds story time from presentation order | Whether the nonlinear structure is legible |
| Disclosure check | What the reader knows about the family catastrophe after each chapter | Compared with the disclosure map |
| Stageability and sound-off | Production reader describes the scene as silent action | Whether the scene is a physical event in clay |

### Enjoyable (do I want more?)

| Instrument | How | Reads |
|---|---|---|
| Engagement log | A cold reader rates pull 1 to 5 per scene or chapter and quotes the cause | Where interest peaks and sags |
| Prediction gap | Reader predicts the next scene or turn; compare with what happens | Surprise versus predictability |
| Laugh and feeling marks | Reader marks quoted lines or beats where they smiled, winced, or felt something, and what kind | Where comedy and emotion are located (weakest instrument; calibrate against humans) |
| Skip test | Would the reader skip this scene on a second viewing? | Dead weight |
| Pairwise preference | Reader chooses between two versions (A or B) and quotes why | More reliable than absolute scores; use when revising |
| Ticket test | Pitch reader says whether they would buy a ticket (1 to 5) and the reason | Premise pull |
| Variety read | Reader lists tone and shape per chapter | Monotony and whiplash |

### Scoring rules

- Use several readers with different stances (casual viewer who knows only the popular Hercules, comedy fan, myth reader, animation fan, skeptic) and report the spread, not the mean.
- Prefer ranking and pairwise comparison over absolute scores. Models compress ratings toward 4.
- Anchor scales with the calibration pair (a scene written to work and a deliberately flat one).
- Never use a vote or average as a gate.

## Tiers and cost

A reviewer call is not free. In the calibration runs each isolated reviewer consumed about 60,000 tokens, mostly fixed overhead, so a six-lens panel costs about 360,000 tokens per run. Run the full panel only where it earns its cost.

| Tier | What | When |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Mechanical checks by script: IDs, scene card present, runtime sums, banned names or terms in opening movements, scene index fields | Every scene, every edit |
| 1 | Two reviewers per scene: cold reader (functional and enjoyable) plus an auditor who combines boundary check and story tests | Every scene draft, batched by sequence where possible |
| 2 | Full panel with all lenses | Key scenes (opening, Megara chapter, midpoint, climax), any scene Tier 1 flags, each sequence, each full draft |
| 3 | Human table read, then the animatic | Per sequence once available; before approval |

Batch several scenes into one reviewer call at sequence level to spread the fixed overhead.

## Scene card

Every scene carries a card. A reviewer infers the card from the pages, and the writer's card is compared with it.

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Purpose | What the story needs from this scene |
| Entering condition | Situation and each principal's want on entry |
| Turn | What changes |
| Exit condition | The new situation |
| Principal physical event | What a viewer sees with the sound off |
| Engine | What keeps it alive (a joke from character, pressure, or both) |

## Scene tests (floor)

1. **Turn.** Something changes for someone.
2. **Want and obstacle.** At least one principal wants something and is blocked.
3. **Delete.** Name the later scene that breaks if this one is cut.
4. **Physical event.** One principal physical event carries the meaning and is followable with the sound off.
5. **No narrated knowledge.** No line exists mainly to tell the audience what both speakers already know.
6. **Blind voice.** With names covered, a reader can tell the principals apart.
7. **Engine.** The scene names what keeps it alive. Comedy comes from character or situation.
8. **Boundaries.** No boundary is crossed.
9. **Stageable in clay.** No unconstrained crowds, continuous transformations without a technique, or invisible thoughts.
10. **Heavy hand.** Passes the heavy-hand test above.

## Sequence and story tests

1. **Causal chain.** Adjacent beats connect with "therefore" or "but", not "and then".
2. **Chronology reconstruction.** A cold reader rebuilds story time.
3. **Disclosure audit.** The family catastrophe is disclosed where the disclosure map says.
4. **Setup and payoff ledger.** Every planted thing is paid or knowingly dropped; no payoff arrives unplanted.
5. **Variety.** Adjacent chapters differ in set-piece type, tone, and energy.
6. **Energy curve.** Pull scores 1 to 5 per chapter; flag runs of three or more flat chapters.
7. **Premise payoff.** The system that cannot be satisfied pays off in the ending; the ending is earned.
8. **Ensemble agency.** Supporting characters and the world drive plot.
9. **Heavy hand.** The structure does not read as a thesis demonstrating its own rules.

## Checkpoints

| When | What runs |
|---|---|
| Synopsis or beat sheet (G003) | Story-level lenses, Tier 2 |
| Each sequence draft (G005) | Scene tests on every scene (Tier 1), Tier 2 on key scenes, sequence tests |
| Full draft (G005) | Story and sequence tests; table read when available |
| Animatic (G010) | Real timing, laughs, and drift |

## Findings and severity

- **High:** breaks a scene or story test or crosses a boundary. Fix before the checkpoint passes, or record an explicit exception.
- **Medium:** weakens a scene; fix or route.
- **Low:** polish.
- Reviewers may report no findings. Do not pad.
- Panel disagreement is information. Record it and let the user decide taste questions.
- Verify any reviewer claim about the myth or canon against `01_research/` before acting.
- Each finding resolves to fixed, rejected with reason, or routed to a named goal.

## Recording

Reviews go under `02_story/reviews/`, one file per checkpoint, named by scope and date. Record panel composition, the version of the pages reviewed, reviewer prompts, findings, and resolutions.
