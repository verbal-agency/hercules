# Story Review Rubric

Status: Draft. Calibrated on one scene pair only (`reviews/calibration/calibration_results.md`): the panel caught all eight planted defects and ranked the flat scene below the working one, but the defects were blatant and the reviewers share one model family. It has not shown it can tell good writing from great. Revise after more use. The panel procedure lives in `skills/review-hercules-script/SKILL.md`.

This rubric defines how story and writing quality is checked for the treatment, beat sheet, and screenplay. It supplements, and does not replace, the goal-level acceptance criteria. The user is the taste authority: reviews inform decisions and never approve anything. "Approved" status still requires the user's explicit record (`PROJECT.md`).

## What this process can and cannot show

- **Can show:** where a cold reader is confused or bored, whether a scene turns, whether voices are distinct, whether canon is violated, whether a beat can be staged in clay, and whether the causal chain holds.
- **Cannot show:** that the writing is good or that the film is enjoyable. Enjoyment is approximated with proxies (engagement, surprise, variety). Table reads with real people and the G010 animatic are the real tests.
- Reviewer output comes from model personas. In calibration they padded findings even on a working scene, and three of them wrongly flagged a myth-consistent detail, so allow "no findings" and verify claims about the sources before acting on them. They tend to flatter, to agree with each other, and to over-explain. Treat their notes as instrument readings, require quotes, and discount praise that cites nothing.

## Scene card

Every scene in the scene index carries a card. A reviewer infers the card from the pages; the writer's card is compared with it.

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Purpose | What the story needs from this scene |
| Entering condition | Situation and each principal's want on entry |
| Turn | What changes (a want met or blocked, knowledge gained, a relationship shifted) |
| Exit condition | The new situation |
| Principal physical event | What a viewer sees with the sound off |
| Comic or tension engine | What keeps it alive (a joke from character, pressure, or both) |

## Scene tests (floor: every scene passes all of these, or the exception is recorded)

1. **Turn.** Something changes for someone. A scene where nothing changes fails.
2. **Want and obstacle.** At least one principal wants something and is blocked.
3. **Delete.** Name the later scene that breaks if this one is cut. If none, cut or merge it.
4. **Physical event.** One principal physical event carries the meaning and is followable with the sound off.
5. **No narrated knowledge.** No line exists mainly to tell the audience what both speakers already know.
6. **Blind voice.** With names covered, a reader can tell the principals apart by dialogue alone.
7. **Comic or tension engine.** The scene names its engine. Comedy comes from character or situation, not added quips.
8. **Canon and tone.** No violation of an active decision (AD-032 to AD-036 and earlier). No joke at the expense of victims, the dead, or grief.
9. **Stageable in clay.** No unconstrained crowds, continuous transformations without a technique, or invisible thoughts.

## Cold read (a measurement, not a pass/fail)

A reader with no canon reads in order and records, per scene: what they think is happening, any confusion or drift, a prediction of the next scene, and engagement 1 to 5. Compare predictions with what happens: a reader who is always right finds the film predictable, and one who is always wrong may be lost.

## Macro tests (beat sheet, sequence, and full draft)

1. **Causal chain.** Each beat connects to the next with "therefore" or "but", not "and then".
2. **Chronology reconstruction.** A cold reader given presentation order rebuilds story time without help.
3. **Disclosure audit.** The family catastrophe is disclosed where and as the disclosure map says.
4. **Setup and payoff ledger.** Every planted object, line, or thread is paid off or knowingly dropped.
5. **Variety.** Adjacent chapters differ in set-piece type, tone, and energy. No run of consecutive scenes shares the same shape.
6. **Energy curve.** Readers score each scene 1 to 5; flag runs of three or more flat scenes.
7. **Premise payoff.** The catch (the system that cannot be satisfied) pays off, and the ending is earned.
8. **Ensemble agency.** Supporting characters and the world drive plot, not only Hercules.

## Checkpoints

| When | What runs |
|---|---|
| Beat sheet (G003) | All macro tests. Cheapest place to fix structure. |
| Each sequence draft (G005) | All scene tests on every scene; cold read; macro tests 1, 4, 5 on the sequence. |
| Full draft (G005) | All macro tests; a table read with real people when available. |
| Animatic (G010) | The real timing, laughs, and drift test. |

## Findings and severity

- **High:** breaks a scene or macro test, or violates canon. Fix before the checkpoint passes, or record an explicit exception.
- **Medium:** weakens a scene; fix or route.
- **Low:** polish; backlog or ignore.
- Panel disagreement is information. Do not average verdicts; record the disagreement and decide.
- Each finding resolves to one of: fixed, rejected with reason, or routed to a named goal. The user decides on taste questions.

## Recording

Review outputs go under `02_story/reviews/`, one file per checkpoint, named by scope and date. Record panel composition, version of the pages reviewed, findings, and resolutions. Keep reviewer prompts with the output so a review can be repeated.
